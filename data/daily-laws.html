Question 1 (Compulsory)20 Marks

(a) A agreed to sell his motorcycle to B for Rs. 60,000. B paid Rs. 5,000 as advance. Before delivery, the motorcycle was destroyed by an accidental fire without any fault of A. B demands refund of Rs. 5,000 and also damages of Rs. 10,000 for loss of bargain. Decide with reference to the doctrine of supervening impossibility and Section 65 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872. (5 Marks) (b) P, a minor, obtained a loan of Rs. 50,000 from Q by falsely representing himself to be a major. Q now sues P for recovery of the loan and, alternatively, seeks to make P liable in tort for deceit. Discuss the legal position of a minor's agreement, the rule of estoppel and the doctrine of restitution. (5 Marks) (c) X sold his running bakery business along with goodwill to Y and agreed not to carry on a similar business anywhere in India for 10 years. Y later finds X running a bakery in another district after 2 years. Examine the validity of the restraint under Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872. (5 Marks) (d) State the essential elements of a valid partnership under the Indian Partnership Act, 1932. Can two partnership firms enter into partnership with each other? (5 Marks)

(a) Contract void u/s 56; refund Rs.5,000 u/s 65, no damages. (b) Void ab initio; no estoppel; no restitution of spent money. (c) Valid if reasonable—goodwill sale exception. (d) Agreement, business, profit-sharing, mutual agency; firms cannot be partners, only individuals.
Question 220 Marks

(a) M offers to sell his laptop to N by a letter posted on 1st March. N posts his acceptance on 5th March. On 4th March M posts a revocation letter which reaches N on 6th March, while N's acceptance reaches M on 8th March. Decide whether a contract exists, explaining the rules of communication and revocation of offer and acceptance under Sections 4 and 5 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872. (6 Marks) (b) Distinguish between a contract of indemnity and a contract of guarantee with at least five points of difference. (6 Marks) (c) 'An agreement without consideration is void, but there are exceptions.' Enumerate and briefly explain the exceptions under Section 25 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872. (8 Marks)

(a) Contract concluded on 5 March; revocation received after acceptance posted—too late; binding. (b) Parties, liability, purpose, request, recovery rights. (c) Natural love, past voluntary services, time-barred debt, agency, gift, bailment, charitable subscription.
Question 320 Marks

(a) A buyer purchased a second-hand car from a dealer after telling him he needed it for touring purposes. The car proved unfit for touring. Discuss the implied conditions as to quality and fitness under Section 16 of the Sale of Goods Act, 1930 and the buyer's remedies. (6 Marks) (b) Explain the rules regarding transfer of property in unascertained goods and goods sent on 'sale or return' basis under the Sale of Goods Act, 1930, with examples. (6 Marks) (c) Who is an 'unpaid seller'? Explain his rights against the goods and against the buyer personally. State whether the right of lien is lost when the seller obtains a decree for the price. (8 Marks)

(a) Fitness condition applies—purpose disclosed, reliance on skill; buyer may reject/claim damages. (b) Property passes on unconditional appropriation with assent; sale-or-return on approval/adoption/lapse of time. (c) Lien, stoppage, resale; suit for price/damages; lien not lost by decree.
Question 420 Marks

(a) Explain the doctrine of 'implied authority' of a partner under Section 19 of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932. List any five acts which are outside a partner's implied authority. (6 Marks) (b) R retires from a firm of three partners without giving public notice. Six months later the firm takes a loan from a bank which believed R to be still a partner. Discuss R's liability for acts done after retirement under Section 32. Also state the position of the estate of a deceased partner. (6 Marks) (c) Distinguish between dissolution of partnership and dissolution of firm. State the grounds on which a court may order dissolution of a firm under Section 44. (8 Marks)

(a) Authority to bind firm in usual business; exclusions—arbitration, compromise, immovable property, partner admission, withdraw suit, acquire property. (b) R liable absent public notice; deceased partner's estate not liable. (c) Firm continues vs ends; grounds—insanity, incapacity, misconduct, breach, transfer, loss, just and equitable.
Question 520 Marks

(a) Explain the concept of 'Limited Liability Partnership' and state any six differences between an LLP and a traditional partnership firm under the LLP Act, 2008. (7 Marks) (b) What is meant by 'designated partner'? State the eligibility conditions and the statutory liabilities of designated partners in an LLP. (6 Marks) (c) Discuss the doctrine of 'lifting of the corporate veil' under the Companies Act, 2013 with reference to at least four circumstances, judicial or statutory, where the veil may be pierced. (7 Marks)

(a) Body corporate, perpetual succession, limited liability, separate legal entity; differences in registration, liability, members, agency. (b) Minimum two, one resident; DPIN; responsible for compliance/penalties. (c) Fraud, tax evasion, enemy character, sham entity, statutory misstatement in prospectus.
Question 620 Marks

(a) Define a 'private company' under the Companies Act, 2013 and state any five privileges available to it as compared to a public company. (6 Marks) (b) Explain the doctrine of 'indoor management' and its exceptions. How does it differ from the doctrine of constructive notice? (7 Marks) (c) A public company issued a prospectus containing an untrue statement about the utilisation of issue proceeds. An investor subscribed relying on it and suffered loss. Discuss the civil and criminal liability for misstatement in a prospectus and the defences available to a director. (7 Marks)

(a) Restricted transfer, max 200 members, no public invitation; privileges—two members, two directors, fewer meeting formalities. (b) Outsiders presume internal compliance; exceptions—knowledge, suspicion, forgery, negligence; contrast constructive notice. (c) Sections 34/35/36 liability; defences—withdrawal of consent, ignorance, reasonable belief.
