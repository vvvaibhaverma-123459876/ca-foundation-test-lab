Full syllabus practice

  1. 1.Business Economics is best described as:
    1. A purely normative science with no positive elements
    2. Application of economic theory and methodology to business decision-making
    3. A branch of accountancy dealing with cost records
    4. The study of only macroeconomic aggregates
    Business economics applies economic theory and analytical tools to solve practical business decision problems.
  2. 2.The central economic problem arises primarily because:
    1. Governments intervene in markets
    2. Wants are unlimited while resources are scarce and have alternative uses
    3. Money supply is fixed
    4. Firms aim to maximise profit
    Scarcity of resources with alternative uses relative to unlimited wants creates the central problem of choice.
  3. 3.A statement that 'the government should reduce GST on essential goods' is an example of:
    1. Positive economics
    2. Normative economics
    3. Descriptive statistics
    4. Econometric forecasting
    Value judgements about what ought to be done belong to normative economics.
  4. 4.In a capitalist economy, the basic economic problems are solved mainly through:
    1. Central planning authority
    2. Price mechanism and market forces
    3. Community traditions
    4. Government budget allocation
    Capitalist economies rely on the price mechanism to allocate resources.
  5. 5.The production possibility curve is concave to the origin because of:
    1. Constant opportunity cost
    2. Increasing marginal opportunity cost
    3. Decreasing returns to money
    4. Perfect substitutability of resources
    Resources are not equally efficient in all uses, so opportunity cost rises, making PPC concave.
  6. 6.Microeconomics is also referred to as:
    1. Income theory
    2. Price theory
    3. Aggregate theory
    4. Growth theory
    Microeconomics studies individual prices and outputs, hence called price theory.
  7. 7.Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of a mixed economy?
    1. Coexistence of public and private sectors
    2. Economic planning alongside markets
    3. Complete absence of private property
    4. Government regulation of key industries
    Mixed economies retain private property; total absence of it characterises socialism.
  8. 8.Opportunity cost of a decision is:
    1. Total money spent on the decision
    2. Value of the next best alternative foregone
    3. Sum of all alternatives foregone
    4. Accounting cost plus taxes
    Opportunity cost measures only the highest-valued alternative sacrificed.
  9. 9.The law of demand states that, other things equal, quantity demanded:
    1. Rises as price rises
    2. Falls as price rises
    3. Is independent of price
    4. Varies with income only

    1. 1.0
    2. 2.0
    3. 0.5
    4. 1.5

    1. Positive income elasticity and negative price elasticity
    2. Strongly negative income effect outweighing substitution effect
    3. Perfectly elastic demand

    1. Negative
    2. Zero

    1. Rise
    2. Fall
    3. Remain unchanged
    4. First rise then fall
